"Whatever the trail you take, may your journeys be joyful, and you live in love."

A Life Shaped by Wilderness

I didn’t grow up thinking I’d become “Mr. Nature.” But looking back, the land was shaping me long before I had the words for it. Texas creeks, shaded prairies, and the quiet pull of the moon taught me early that the world speaks — if you’re willing to listen. I learned to read landscapes before I learned to read books. I learned that water is a teacher, storms are honest, and the wilderness doesn’t lie to you the way people sometimes do.

I’ve spent years guiding across rivers, canyons, forests, and mountains — but the truth is, the wilderness has been guiding me far longer.

The Trail That Changed Everything

In 2019, I stepped onto the Appalachian Trail with muddy feet, a friend named Sam, and a body already aching from the first 26 miles. The rain didn’t stop — not for a while — but the trail taught me that clarity doesn’t always come from sunlight. Sometimes it comes from walking through the storm long enough to see what’s on the other side.

I learned generosity from strangers, resilience from broken gear, humility from steep climbs, and joy from sunsets I waited hours to see — even if it meant hiking six miles in the dark afterward. I learned that doubt fades into determination. That the trail family is real. That the wilderness is a mirror — and sometimes you don’t like what you see until you’ve walked far enough to understand it.

Guiding With Heart

Years later, I found myself guiding youth in the deserts of Utah — not hikers chasing summits, but young people wrestling with their own storms. I’ve watched a reluctant teenager refuse food and water, only to accept a leather heart pouch the next morning — not as a symbol of commitment to a program, but as a symbol of commitment to himself.

I’ve watched families try to mend years of hurt in a two‑day campout. I’ve watched fire ceremonies melt walls that therapy alone couldn’t touch. I’ve watched wilderness soften the jaded and strengthen the weary. Guiding isn’t just logistics — it’s stewardship, relational work, and spiritual presence.

A Philosophy Rooted in Nature

I write — a lot. Sometimes in journals, sometimes in long trail posts typed furiously on an iPhone, sometimes in sprawling philosophical scripts about zero points, watersheds, and the strange ways modern culture misunderstands nature. My teachings aren’t academic — they’re lived.

I believe we begin at zero — the point of balance between infinities. I believe nature is the original teacher. I believe hard days are compost — they break down so new growth can emerge. I believe joy is found in moments, not milestones. And I believe love is the only sustainable way to move through the world.

Experience That Grounds the Spirit

I’ve led 21‑day intensives, multi‑day expeditions, weekend retreats, crash courses, wilderness therapy groups, youth programs, and community‑based service projects. I’ve directed summer camps, managed teams of 70, overseen million‑dollar programs, founded a community aid organization, and facilitated thousands of overnight stays.

But none of that matters as much as this: I guide with heart. I guide with intention. I guide with respect for the land and the people who walk it with me.
